# Worldwide Jimny modification evidence and coverage priorities

Research checked: **22 October 2026**. The baseline below records the initial research inspection. Subsequent implementation added 17 product records (322 total), 13 regional directory entries and four distinct representative part models. A searchable 20-category version of this guide is available in Owner Tools. Source recurrence is not a measured worldwide sales ranking.

## What the evidence supports

There is a broad, real aftermarket for heritage/retro appearance, wheels and tyres, compact touring/camping equipment, protection and mechanical upgrades. These categories recur across independent regional suppliers and genuine-accessory catalogues. This is **not a worldwide sales ranking**: catalogue breadth, a shop's “popular” section, owner posts and manufacturer marketing do not establish global popularity or independent quality.

The most useful next step is better coverage of distinct, correctly fitted variants, not another large undifferentiated import of seller listings. An accurate OEM-plus build, a lightweight tourer, a compact protected trail build and a Japanese retro build would provide clearer exploration paths. Those are proposed editorial groupings, not measured market segments.

### Valuable modifications that should not produce a fake exterior transformation

- Suspension coils/dampers, zero-rise kits, caster/panhard correction: source 6,12,27,28. Geometry should reflect measured ride height only, and handling simulation must not imply validated real-world performance.
- Differential lockers/LSD, breathers and control kits: source 4,6,10. Use an informative specification/caution panel; no invented visible change or unvalidated traction benefit.
- Auxiliary fuel tanks, airboxes, internal mounts and wiring: source 7. Show underbody/exploded detail only when actually modeled; otherwise label as research-only.
- Floor mats, audio, filters, interior trim and convenience storage: source 1,8,24,28. These deserve useful sourcing even without an exterior preview.
- Recovery points, guards and protection can be subtle rather than absent. Offer an optional detail camera, but do not equate appearance with a certified load rating.

### Data rules

1. Keep separate records for **product**, **seller offer**, **fitter** and **community resource**. One manufacturer product can have many regional seller offers; this should not inflate “all parts” or generate repeated thumbnails.
2. Store source URL, checked date, manufacturer/part number, body style, model/year/type, market, finish, required mounts, conflicts, stock-at-check and verification confidence. Preserve contradictory evidence for review.
3. Add a rendering status: **exact-source model**, **representative shape**, **colour-only preview**, or **not visualised**. The two reference-guided records currently present are not automatically manufacturer-accurate.
4. Never infer five-door compatibility from a broad collection or import-country label. Japan JB64, Sierra JB74 and Nomade JC74 are distinct source labels; regional chassis naming and revisions still require item-level confirmation.
5. Maintain local and import options separately. A regional seller list needs currency, dispatch region, fitting availability and an honest “confirm availability” state, not fabricated delivery times or global stock.
6. Expose cosmetic versus functional categories. Side garnish is not a rock slider; an ornamental hook is not a rated recovery point; a roof platform is not permission to exceed roof/payload limits.
7. Photo/CAD/image licensing was **not verified** here. Request supplier media permission, use authorised feeds, or use original accurately labeled renders. Keep owner photo consent and attribution records for community submissions. Do not assume a source citation licenses its photography.
8. Regional legal/inspection/insurance rules require a separate current authoritative review before giving jurisdiction-specific advice. This report is product research, not legal or engineering approval.
