# Build & Trail: scope, provenance and limitations

Implemented 20 October 2026. Independent educational game, not a Suzuki-approved simulator or driver qualification. Four-speed automatic K15B wide-body Jimny only; no kei JB64 or manual-clutch dynamics. Both existing authored body assets are reused. The supporting 4x4s are original, unbranded scenery: a compact two-door and a longer expedition vehicle, evoking the broader Thar/Wrangler category without claiming accurate licensed models.

## Primary references

- Suzuki owner manual 78RB1-01E, English, hosted by Suzuki Israel: https://suzuki.co.il/sites/default/files/pdf/jimny_new_english.pdf . Read transfer operation pp. 3-19–22, warnings p. 3-18, hill descent pp. 3-59–61 and hill hold p. 3-62. The game represents 2H / 4H / 4L ordering, stopped + Neutral low-range changes for automatic, straight front wheels/no wheelspin for transfer changes, dry-pavement restriction, HDC activation below 25 km/h in a driving gear, approximate 10/5 km/h high/low-range descent targets. Conservative game restrictions are labelled as game restrictions. It does not model every cold-weather/shift fault, indicator state or assistance failure.
- Suzuki Australia specifications: https://www.suzuki.com.au/vehicles/4x4/jimny/specifications/ . K15B 1462 cc, peak 130 Nm at 4000 rpm; 4AT ratios 2.875 / 1.568 / 1 / .697, reverse 2.3, final drive 4.3. Public figures vary by generation/market; these are not a complete vehicle calibration.
- Body dimensions and authored asset provenance: ASSET_SOURCES.md . Wheelbases 2.250 / 2.590 m, stock 195/80 R15 radius .3465 m. Collision/contact geometry is simpler than the visual mesh.

## Explicit simulation assumptions

21 October open-world revision: the live game uses Rapier 0.19.3 at a fixed 60 Hz with a dynamic rigid chassis, terrain heightfield, obstacle/camp collision hulls and four ray-cast wheel/suspension contacts. Steering, driveline force, braking impulses, low-range gearing, surface grip, drag, coupled-axle anti-roll force and HDC affect that body. Wheel centres, ground-contact flags, suspension lengths and wheel rotations are read from the solver. The old planar model remains a labelled initialization fallback and regression reference, not the primary engine. Rapier documentation: https://rapier.rs/javascript3d/classes/DynamicRayCastVehicleController.html and https://rapier.rs/docs/user_guides/javascript/rigid_bodies/ .

Masses including a nominal driver (1190/1290 kg), torque curve outside its published peak, converter creep, automatic shift map, transfer reduction approximation (1.32 high; low approximately twice high), grip coefficients, spring/damper tuning, centre of gravity, inertia and brake thermal behaviour are **not measured Jimny data**. Rigid-body physics does not make this a validated tyre/suspension model: it uses ray-cast wheels, simplified body collision hulls and an approximate coupled-axle response, not mechanically modelled live axles. No aerodynamic validation, driveline wind-up physics, exact electronic-control replication, tyre pressure/deformation, water depth, winching, damage, manual clutch or calibrated rollover behaviour is claimed. Recovery remains a game reset, not training for real recovery.

Learn / Trail / Expert choose different authored routes, articulation-ramp heights, checkpoint tolerance and coaching. Vehicle grip is determined by surface, not secretly changed by difficulty. Twelve missions occupy three open square environments: Himachal (600 m), Rajasthan (800 m) and the urban proving ground (320 m); Control School uses the same proving-ground world. Routes include stop/reverse/drive-range requirements, navigational loops, rock lanes, a crest, a descent, parking and slalom. The world perimeter is marked; driving off a suggested route does not teleport the car. Points reward control, not the shortest elapsed time. Mechanical settings remain representative stock; visual aftermarket selections do not claim measured handling gains.

Courses are fictional closed environments inspired by India, not mapped roads or safe real-world routes. World geometry, buildings, signs, foliage and rocks are original; the ground photographs are licensed as described below. Main site never imports the game bundle. Storage is local-only; no accounts or analytics. See the current audio implementation notes for sound provenance; no authentic Jimny recording is claimed.

21 October coaching/scenery pass: ground material uses locally hosted 1K CC0 Poly Haven Brown Mud / Brown Mud Leaves 01 photographs and a normal map; see `assets/trail-textures/LICENSE.md` for exact sources. These are repeating surface textures, not photographic backgrounds. Ridgelines, track geometry and vegetation are original. Offset rut heights feed the same four wheel samples as rendered geometry. Soft-shoulder sand uses assumed friction 0.22 and rolling resistance 0.095 versus ordinary sand 0.40 / 0.055, not measured Jimny/terrain values. No individual wheel burial, tyre deformation or validated stuck-wheel diagnosis is simulated.

Spoken coaching offers 24 locally hosted Cillian recordings generated through Higgsfield after explicit approval of the 19.4-credit quote. Exact scripts and job provenance are in VOICE_PRODUCTION.md and assets/audio/coaching/production.json. No provider request or credential runs in the client; custom/unrecorded instructions can fall back to an optional local English Web Speech voice. Captions, repeat/mute and unavailable-audio messaging remain. Transfer instructions retain the manual-based stop/Neutral sequence; spotter advice does not teach winching, jacking or unsupervised recovery. Inspection pauses simulation and offers all four wheel contacts, front/side/overhead angles. Automatic inspection is limited to low-speed contact or persistent commanded motion without progress; it is optional and cooldown-limited. Wheel-contact flags now come from the rigid solver. Aggregate slip is still not individual-wheel tyre slip, and a contact loss is not a diagnosis of wheel burial.

Cabin-control pass: the authored steering-wheel geometry is separated from the runtime GLB copy. Both Blender masters now include a closed floor/firewall, inset sills, seat seams, instrument details and material UVs. Deterministic original fabric/plastic microtextures are applied locally. The selector, transfer and parking-brake animations, dial sweeps and 14:1 visual steering ratio are representative teaching aids, not measured factory control travel. The earlier oversized training display has been removed. State reflects accepted gear/range changes, including rejected-shift guards. A 384×240 cabin thumbnail is rendered from the same scene at up to 10 Hz. First-person view hides only the driver's head to avoid clipping; the body, steering hands and feet remain visible. Mirrors still use environmental reflections, not functional rear-view cameras. This is not a scan-level cabin replica.

## Driving feel layer (10 November 2026)

Guidance and feedback only; the vehicle model, grip values, shift guards and the score formula are unchanged. The active checkpoint has a light beacon and an on-screen marker with distance that slides to the screen edge when the gate is out of view. Loose ground and sand raise wheel dust from the solver's wheel positions and aggregate slip; it is a cosmetic particle effect, not a simulation of soil or visibility. The chase camera widens slightly above 25 km/h and shakes on impacts, both disabled under reduced-motion preferences. Standard-layout controllers are supported through the browser Gamepad API with analog steering, throttle and brake; buttons call the same guarded selector, transfer and parking-brake actions as the keyboard, and unknown layouts are ignored. Rumble and phone vibration are optional browser features and may be absent. The results screen now lists where control-score points were dropped (speed target, impacts, wheelspin, side lean, 4WD on tarmac, recoveries), reconstructed from the same penalties that produce the score. Time trials store checkpoint splits for the personal best on this device and show the difference at each gate. Automatic graphics lowers render resolution, then shadow resolution, only when the measured frame rate stays low; Sharp and Fast override it from the pause menu. `tests/trail-feel.test.mjs` covers the marker maths, controller mapping, score breakdown, resolution governor and dust rates. Frame rate on real phones and controllers was not measured in this pass; only headless software rendering was available.

## Himachal Adventure style (10 November 2026)

Himachal now opens in Adventure style, an arcade layer over the same rigid-body world; Simulator style keeps every guarded control and the control score. Adventure is **not** a representation of a stock Jimny: engine force is multiplied by 1.75 and tyre grip by 1.15, the everyday pedal is the full pedal, the selector and transfer lever are automated (the brake reverses once stopped; 2H on paved road, 4H elsewhere, 4L for slow steep work) and none of the stop, Neutral or range interlocks apply. Checkpoints lose their stop, range, gear, reverse and wheel-placement conditions and become drive-through gates. Runs are timed after a three-second countdown; a recovery adds five seconds, a rollover recovers automatically, and each gold token removes half a second. Star times are about 1.2× and 1.65× the time of the autopilot in `scripts/adventure-times.mjs` (40 km/h cruise) and have not been tuned against human players. That script completed all eight missions and the chained grand traverse at every difficulty for both bodies without a recovery; it is a feasibility check, not evidence that the driving feels good.

The chase camera now smooths its orbit angle instead of its position, so its distance no longer stretches under acceleration; it sits about 6.4–8 m behind (previously 10 m plus lag), lifts over crests that would hide the car and zooms with the scroll wheel. A Wheels camera frames one corner at ground level with the wheel ringed (red when the solver reports no contact). In Adventure it cuts in automatically after about a second of throttle without progress and returns once the car is moving. The framed wheel is the airborne one, otherwise the most compressed corner: a pointer for the player, not a diagnosis.

Trail names cite real Himachal areas only as inspiration (Gramphu–Batal, Chandra valley, Hampta, Sach Pass, Jalori Pass, Kunzum La, Rohtang). The terrain, routes and distances are fictional and unchanged. Roadside prayer-flag lines, chortens and yellow-capped distance stones are original procedural geometry; flags carry no text or imagery, and stone labels show fictional in-game distances. Only the chortens have collision.

## Himachal play world, second pass (10 November 2026)

This supersedes the event list, tokens and recovery rules in the section above. Adventure now offers three events (grand traverse, pass road, rock garden) instead of nine trails; Simulator keeps every mission. Adventure builds its own variant of the Himachal world (`makeWorld(course, difficulty, {adventure: true})`); the Simulator world, its closed rivers and its guarded banks are unchanged and still pass `scripts/trail-reachability.mjs`.

In the play variant every rock obstacle is lowered to at most 0.46 of its radius and collides as a shallow spherical cap (about 45° at its foot) so it can be driven over; this is a game shape, not a claim about what a Jimny can climb. Both rivers can be entered. The highland river's water level drops from 1.2 m to 0.9 m and its retaining walls are removed, and the river now runs off both edges of the map in every world instead of stopping 45 m short. Water adds drag in proportion to depth and throws spray; there is no buoyancy, current, flooding or wading-depth limit, and none is implied for a real vehicle. The last 16 m before the map boundary add heavy drag ("scree") ahead of the physical wall. Recover in Adventure and in free roam returns the car to its own position about 1.6 s earlier (upright, all wheels down), with the same five-second cost, instead of the last checkpoint or the start.

Gold tokens are replaced by apples: at most seven per event, each placed 6–12 m to one side of the route on ground that is not steep, not inside an obstacle and not in deep water, worth two seconds. The rock garden keeps its wheel-placement gates, so its steps cannot be bypassed, and Adventure defaults to the Trail obstacle size.

The pass road is reshaped for every style: alternating mid-points 7 m off line turn its straights into S-bends, and the road and its hillside are scaled vertically by 1.28 about the anchor height (gravel about 9.6%, paved about 5.9%; the gravel climb now gains roughly 70 m). The western drop beside the upper gravel road is steep and unfenced.

Night mode (Himachal only) swaps the daylight preset for moonlight, hides the sky dome behind a star field and switches on two headlight spot lamps and a tail lamp. Lamps are an artistic approximation without shadows or a measured beam pattern. The engine sound uses the same five orders as before but as sine tones weighted lower, under a 1.4 kHz low-pass, at roughly 8 dB less overall; it remains synthesized and is not a Jimny recording.

Rendering now blends the last two 60 Hz physics poses, which removes judder on displays that do not tick in step with the simulation; it changes nothing in the simulation. An earth curtain under the playable edge closes the cracks between the fine playable ground and the coarser outer landform. Terrain heights are computed once per world and shared by the ground mesh and the physics heightfield (identical sample points), and are computed while the model downloads. In a headless software-GL run this cut the time from pressing Drive to moving from about 3.9 s to 1.6 s; the scene build itself is still several seconds of main-thread work and was not reduced much.

## Himachal challenges and lighting, third pass (10 November 2026)

Adventure has five events of different kinds: the grand traverse and pass road (checkpoint runs), the rock garden (wheel placement), a river run and an apple hunt. The river run enters the section stream at a lower ford, drives up the stream bed and leaves at an upper ford before crossing back. The apple hunt has no route: ten apples are scattered on both banks in a fixed pattern and may be collected in any order, with the marker and beacon on the nearest; the stream must be crossed by the bridge or a ford. Hunt apples carry no time bonus because the time is the score.

Two fords exist only in the play world (`SECTION_FORDS`, z = 158 and 188). The west bank becomes a long ramp. The east bank is cut to one even slope that ends at the edge of the riverside lane, which is left at its original height; an earlier version that reached the lane tipped it sideways and made the autopilot fall off, so the lane is now asserted unchanged by a test. Bank rocks are omitted at the fords, thin marker posts and the bridge's rail posts no longer collide in the play world (the rails still do), and "FORD" stones mark both banks. Fording depth, drag and bank angles are game values, not guidance for a real crossing.

Time of day (Himachal only) is Day, Golden hour or Night. Golden hour lowers and warms the sun for long shadows and switches the lamps on dimly. At night the headlights now fall off with distance^1.5 instead of the physical inverse square and are aimed about 45 m ahead, which roughly triples the light reaching 30 m; the night fog also starts and ends further out. That is deliberately non-physical so the road is readable at speed. Visible beam cones, lamp glows, brake and reversing glows are additive sprites with no measured photometry. Light snow flurries appear when the camera is more than about 46 m up, reaching full strength near the pass; there is no snow on the ground and no change to grip.

`scripts/adventure-times.mjs` now also recovers like a player (staying within 8 m for 20 s) and steers round rock clusters. It finished all five events at three difficulties for both bodies with no recoveries. Star times remain about 1.15–1.2× its time.

## Verification

Player/share update, 21 October 2026: optional man/woman avatars use the pinned CC0 MakeHuman base, shape targets and rig/weights described in DRIVER_SOURCES.md, with authored seated poses, clothing colours and steering arm IK. These are generic anatomical game characters, not scans or real-person likenesses; skin, hair and fingers remain simplified. The older procedural shapes are retained only as a model-load fallback. Optional names are locally rendered text projected onto both front doors; no third-party decal graphics or likenesses are used. Recaps record actual simulated state at 20Hz, including wheel centres/rotations, and re-render those samples from tracking/close-up cameras. This adds no claim of validated physics. MP4/WebM exports can include synchronized procedural game audio and the recorded instructor clips actually heard, respecting interruptions and clip boundaries; local system speech is not captured. Local JPEG stills remain available. Nothing is uploaded.

Audio is originally synthesized with Web Audio: RPM/load-driven engine harmonics, filtered tyre/scrub/wind/environment noise, contact/shift cues, interior filtering and coaching ducking. It is **not a recording or a measured acoustic model of a Jimny**. Independent mix controls and mute are provided. Exports clone audio tracks so recording cleanup does not stop the live game's audio destination.

Higgsfield was inspected first through the connected plugin, then through signed-in Safari after explicit user permission. Its model catalogue lists Meshy GLB generators, but this session did not expose the corresponding `generate_3d` tool. Safari confirmed a scene GLB export option; it did not establish a compatible, priced direct-text human-generation workflow or guaranteed skeleton export. No 3D-generation job or paid vehicle/human asset was submitted. Scene editing/export is not equivalent to the missing generation command. The separately approved coaching audio was generated through the plugin, not the browser.

The game also reuses the project's previously generated India-inspired panoramas as distant background scenery. See SCENERY_SOURCES.md. These are AI-generated raster backdrops, not photographic, mapped or navigable terrain. Terrain-menu previews are screenshots of the actual game renderer, not new concept images.

`tests/trail-game.test.mjs` covers transfer/selector guards, dry tarmac, HDC eligibility and response, braking, gearing, wheelbase, fixed-step stability, course gates and scoring. Browser testing must also cover desktop keyboard/touch controls, pause/recovery, both body loads, model errors and mobile viewport overflow. The result remains an openly labelled beta; OEM-equivalent physics needs measured vehicle data and specialist validation.

The new `tests/trail-worlds.test.mjs` covers square world extents, twelve mission definitions, difficulty-specific routes and actual ramp geometry, stop/reverse/range checkpoint conditions, input mappings, rigid-body steering/braking, obstacle collisions, off-route exploration, handbrake, reverse, Neutral, heightfield orientation, airborne wheels, HDC, low-range gearing and repeated recovery stability. `node scripts/trail-reachability.mjs` completes all 36 mission/difficulty combinations using actual physics and guarded gear/range operations, including steering around boulders. On 21 October all 36 completed for each body: 72/72 combinations in total. This is an automated feasibility check, **not** proof of natural player comprehension, visual fidelity or enjoyable challenge; those need browser/human playthroughs. `BODY`, `COURSE`, `MISSION`, `DIFFICULTY` and `DEBUG` environment filters can isolate a failure.
