Overrides persist in full builds, share links and the driving renderer; saved parts kits carry their selected hardware finishes without globally repainting the car. These cosmetic previews are not coating/fitment advice or costed quotes.

## Development and release

### Sharing and player identity

**Save build image** sits directly beneath the configurator. **Layout: Guide · try Studio** switches between the two desktop layouts without replacing the build. Studio is a wider car-first layout, not a separate application.

In Build & Trail, optional player names, animated man/woman drivers and front-door name decals are set in the course menu and stored locally. Pause or finish a course, then choose **Replay & share**. Recaps use the latest 15/30/60 seconds available, with cinematic, wheel-detail or chase edits in landscape, portrait or square. Actual simulated vehicle poses, steering and wheel heights are recorded at 20Hz in a two-minute memory buffer. Changing course clears that buffer. Recovery jumps are not interpolated. Replay does not resimulate or alter the driving score.

Videos can include engine/terrain effects and the recorded instructor, independently selectable. Local device speech is not captured. Video uses canvas capture and MediaRecorder with MP4/WebM capability detection; no cloud encoding, upload, account or microphone permission. Keep the tab visible while exporting; cancellation restores the paused live view. Unsupported browsers retain still-image download. Drivers use CC0 MakeHuman anatomy and rig data with authored seated poses, clothing and steering IK; see `modeling/DRIVER_SOURCES.md`. They remain game characters, not scans or real-person likenesses. `src/trail/replay.js`, `src/trail/voice-replay.js` and `src/media-export.js` handle bounded replay state, narration timing and exports.

### Build & Trail driving lab (local beta)

Open `game.html` or choose **Drive & learn** in the main header. Three fictional open environments cover Himachal, Rajasthan and a proving ground, with twenty missions plus four separate lessons and free roam. All three worlds retain threefold roaming area. Four optional **Pro trials** in the proving ground test an elevated S-bridge, alternating staircase, momentum climb/summit stop and reverse pocket. Their dimensions stay fixed across difficulties; their gates validate elevation, tyre contact, range, selector and applicable speed/alignment requirements. Most other Learn/Trail/Expert missions change routes, ramp heights, requirements and coaching, not physical grip. Both Jimny bodies use a Rapier rigid chassis and four ray-cast wheel/suspension contacts with an approximate four-speed automatic driveline, guarded 2H/4H/4L changes, descent assistance and recovery. The game imports saved paint, decals, supported visual parts and names, but keeps mechanical settings stock. Support vehicles are original unbranded scenery, not accurate licensed competitor models.

This is a first playable educational beta, not OEM physics or qualified off-road training. Published dimensions/gear ratios anchor a simplified fixed-step vehicle model; suspension, tyres and electronic systems are approximate. There is no manual-clutch training, true rigid-body rollover/damage, calibrated aftermarket performance or multiplayer. Read `modeling/GAME_SOURCES.md` for control sources and assumptions. Course durations are estimates; thoughtful first-time play is intended to fill roughly 10–15 minutes, with unlimited replays and no timer pressure.

Game code is isolated in `src/trail-game.js` and `src/trail/`, bundled separately to `assets/trail-game.js` by `npm run build`. The configurator does not fetch the game bundle. `game.css` supplies responsive keyboard/touch controls. Browser verification covers a complete tutorial/result/replay cycle and touch-size layouts, not physical phones or Safari.

No microphone or runtime cloud voice service is used; unavailable speech falls back to text. **Show me** pauses the drive for front, side, overhead and four wheel close-ups. Automatic spotter inspection for persistent low-speed contact/lack of progress can be disabled in coaching settings. The spotter reports simulated ground/contact evidence, not a claim that an individual tyre is buried or wedged. Inspection preserves time, position and score.
